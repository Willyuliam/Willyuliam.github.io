Machine Learning for Protein Modeling

Jun 2026 - Sep 2026 | Advisor: Sorin Istrail (Professor)

I investigated how machine learning can support coarse-grained protein modeling through two related lines of work: a team course project on force-field transferability and a separate research study on contact-persistence-weighted Gaussian network models (GNMs). The work connects graph representations, molecular-dynamics data, physical models, and reproducible statistical evaluation.

  • Course project leadership and method analysis: Led a three-member project, organized the research argument, and integrated the final report and presentation. Developed the CGSchNet section, explaining continuous-filter graph convolutions, energy-based force matching, physical priors, and the distinction between a reusable architecture and demonstrated transfer of trained parameters.
  • Independent source-data analysis: Reanalyzed published processed data for 27 ubiquitin mutations at 21 residue positions and fluctuation profiles for 1ENH and 2A3D. Applied 10,000-replicate bootstrap analyses, residue-cluster resampling, leave-one-out sensitivity, profile shape/scale comparisons, and structure localization to assess ranking, calibration, and local agreement. This analysis used published outputs and did not involve retraining the force field or rerunning molecular simulations.
  • ATLAS data and evaluation pipeline: Built a frozen benchmark of 200 protein chains with three MD replicas per chain, reconstructing residue-level RMSF and continuous contact-persistence labels. Used an ECOD-component-disjoint 132/38/30 train/validation/test split, checked label quality against official ATLAS outputs, and locked model selection before opening the test set.
  • Machine learning with an analytic physical model: Compared geometric baselines, histogram gradient boosting (HGB), edge MLPs, and message-passing neural networks for contact-persistence prediction. Mapped predicted persistence to nonnegative GNM couplings and solved the network for residue flexibility, with direct neural RMSF prediction, oracle weights, and feature/cutoff ablations as comparison methods.
  • Held-out results: On 30 test proteins, the validation-selected HGB model reached protein-macro MAE 0.0856 and Spearman 0.8878 for persistence. The locked weighted GNM increased mean RMSF Spearman from 0.7170 to 0.7229 over uniform GNM. The mean paired effect across 28 ECOD test components was 0.00536 (95% bootstrap CI 0.00126-0.00962; Holm-adjusted sign-flip p = 0.0173), supporting a modest improvement within this benchmark.
  • Reproducible research: Maintained auditable preprocessing, machine-readable results, source hashes, model-selection records, and a local experiment archive. Prepared the course report and a separate research manuscript, distinguishing the locked primary results from subsequent exploratory analyses.
