Policy-Guided Mango Shape Annealing

Apr 2026 - Now | Advisor: John H. Reif (Professor) | Code: GitHub

This project studies how to improve Mango's constrained grammar search for wireframe DNA origami design. Instead of presenting the work as a generic end-to-end RL optimizer, I frame the main contribution as policy-guided proposal selection: the original Mango grammar operators, constraint checks, and annealing-style acceptance logic are preserved, while rule, site, and feasibility choices are guided by masked policies and feasibility-aware proposal scores.

  • Research framing: Refined the project direction from full-action reinforcement learning to masked, constraint-aware proposal guidance. This framing is more defensible because Mango's search space is highly structured: many grammar actions are illegal at a given state, and many legal-looking edits still fail scaffold, edge-length, face-angle, or manufacturability constraints.
  • Hierarchical action space: Built an explicit grammar-action interface over three layers: rule selection, site selection, and parameter selection. The implementation records legal mask size, selected action legality, constraint pass/failure reason, objective change, best-so-far objective, graph hash, scaffold margin, edge-length margin, face-angle margin, and elapsed time for reproducible diagnosis.
  • Policy variants and baselines: Compared PG-Mango variants against Mango-ShapeAnneal, Random-Feasible, Rule-Site, feasibility-filtered rule-site methods, Torch full-action RL, and NoMask ablations. The most stable PG-Mango methods used feasibility-guided proposal scoring, while learned feasibility was evaluated as a promising upgrade path rather than claimed as the final best method.
  • B1-family validation: Ran five B1-family benchmarks with disjoint training and evaluation seeds. PG-Mango beat Mango-ShapeAnneal on 5/5 benchmarks and Random-Feasible on 5/5 benchmarks. Against Random-Feasible, the best PG variant was statistically significant on all five B1-family benchmarks with Mann-Whitney tests at p < 0.05.
  • B2/B3 interpretation: Used B2 restored porosity-variation as the main complex benchmark confirmation, where PG-Mango-Feasibility-Empirical achieved the best mean objective. Used B3 restored manufacturability as a constraint-wall analysis rather than a victory benchmark, identifying Invalid Edge Length as the dominant failure mode and documenting where the search still needs better constraint handling.
  • Feasibility and manufacturability audit: Evaluated a learned feasibility classifier with strong predictive signal (AUC about 0.94 on B1-family, 0.89 on B2, and 0.92 on B3), while noting that integrated learned feasibility did not yet consistently outperform heuristic or empirical variants. Candidate audits confirmed positive scaffold, edge-length, and face-angle margins for top candidates.
  • Result boundary: The defensible conclusion is not that RL solves the whole design problem end to end. The stronger claim is that learning as proposal guidance is more effective than unconstrained full-action RL for Mango's constrained grammar search under sparse feedback, especially when legal action masks and feasibility diagnostics are kept in the loop.
